# v0.51.0 Phase 2 Primitive Execution Review

Date: 2026-09-14

## Result

Phase 2 is complete. Primitive invocation facts and produced-computation safety now use an explicit
runtime-contract vocabulary aligned with checking's execution-fact model. Target validation remains
the sole authority that binds source declarations to primitive roles and compares their contracts.

## Findings and Corrections

`PrimitiveEffects` stored allocation, synchronous waiting, and future-drive certification as three
booleans. The third boolean described a different temporal scope from the first two but the type did
not express that distinction. It also let consumers discuss "effects" without stating whether they
meant primitive invocation or the produced computation.

The runtime contract now publishes `PrimitiveExecutionFacts`. Invocation retains its independent
allocation and synchronous-wait observations, while `PrimitiveProducedComputation` explicitly
classifies either no deferred result or a drive-safe future. Target validation compares this typed
classification with the closed primitive signature. A future signature without the certificate, or
a certificate attached to a non-future signature, remains one integrity failure.

## Boundary Review

- Checking does not depend on `nocter-runtime-contract` and cannot select primitive roles.
- Runtime contracts do not depend on declarations, checking, target closure, or source syntax.
- Target validation receives both authorities and performs the comparison once.
- MIR and machine consumers receive selected primitive identities and cannot reclassify execution.
- The primitive registry still owns one exhaustive role match; source names and backend instruction
  sequences do not participate.

The review found no second primitive allocation, wait, or drive-safety table. Target-service
synchronous-wait facts remain properties of their typed service descriptors rather than primitive
roles and are validated at their existing target-service boundary.

## Evidence

- all `nocter-runtime-contract` unit tests;
- all `nocter-target-program` unit tests;
- all `nocter-checking` unit tests;
- workspace all-target checking and warnings review.

No public language behavior changed in this phase.
